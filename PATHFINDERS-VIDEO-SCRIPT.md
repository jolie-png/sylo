# Sylo — Demo Video Script (2:00)

**Format:** Screen recording with voiceover. No face cam needed.
**Tone:** Calm, direct, first-person. Talking, not reading.
**Pace:** ~150 wpm = ~300 words total.

---

## STRUCTURE

| Section | Time | On screen |
|---------|------|-----------|
| Hook | 0:00–0:12 | Text or real screenshot |
| The gap | 0:12–0:25 | Transition text → homepage |

| Dashboard + dependency chain | 0:35–1:10 | Gap analysis, top move, expand chain on steps |

## SCRIPT

### [0:00–0:12] HOOK

*Screen: A Reddit post screenshot — r/premed or r/csMajors. Title: "How do people find these programs?" or "Sophomore and just found out about Google STEP — am I too late?" Upvotes, comments visible.*

> "Every week, a student realizes they missed something — a program, a deadline, an application window that closed months ago. Not because they weren't qualified. Because nobody told them it existed — or that it was a prerequisite for something else."

---

### [0:12–0:25] THE GAP

*Screen: Fade to simple text on dark background: "Career platforms list opportunities. Nobody models the dependencies between them."*

> "Career platforms tell you what's out there. Your school tells you what's required to graduate. But nobody models the dependencies — which opportunity requires which, what unlocks what, and what order actually works for one student at one school. That's what Sylo does."

---

### [0:25–0:35] PRODUCT INTRO

*Screen: Sylo homepage. Cursor moves to "Try an instant demo →" hover dropdown → clicks "Maya · CS @ Georgia Tech".*

> "A student picks a destination. Sylo reads their major, year, and school — and builds a dependency graph of real opportunities. Not a list. A sequence where every step knows what it requires and what it unlocks. Let me show you."

---

### [0:35–1:10] DASHBOARD — THE DEPENDENCY CHAIN

*Screen: Maya's dashboard loads (CS freshman @ Georgia Tech → Software Engineer). Slow scroll through: (1) gap analysis section, (2) highest-leverage next move card — PURA Salary Award with deadline pill glowing amber "21d left", (3) the Cascade Panel showing Upstream → Unlocks → Window chain on the hero card.*

> "This is Maya — a CS freshman at Georgia Tech heading toward software engineer. Her top move: apply for a PURA Salary Award. Deadline — three weeks away. She qualifies right now, she just didn't know it existed."

*Screen: Pause cursor on the CascadePanel. Highlight: "Requires: Faculty mentor identified." Then: "Unlocks: Research Option credit → GRIP eligibility → Security clearance pipeline." Then: "Window: Sept 15 — one shot per semester."*

> "But look at the chain. PURA requires a faculty mentor — which she gets from joining a lab through UROC, the step above. And PURA unlocks GRIP — government research that requires active research experience. Miss PURA and the next two steps in her roadmap become inaccessible."

*Screen: Click the dependency chain toggle (GitBranch icon) on step 3 (CREATE-X Startup Lab). It expands to show: Requires: None. Unlocks: CREATE-X Launch eligibility → Portfolio project → Working prototype. Then click the chain on step 4 (CREATE-X Launch). It shows: Requires: Completed Startup Lab.*

> "Every step carries this. Startup Lab unlocks Startup Launch — you literally can't do Launch without completing Lab first. This isn't a list you browse. It's a prerequisite graph, and the order is computed."

---

### [1:10–1:25] REORDER WARNING — PROOF THE ENGINE IS STRUCTURAL

*Screen: Drag CREATE-X Launch (step 4) above CREATE-X Startup Lab (step 3). An amber warning banner slides in: "CREATE-X Launch requires CREATE-X Startup Lab — moving it above may break the sequence."*

> "Watch what happens when I drag a step out of order. Sylo warns me — because it knows Launch depends on Lab. A list doesn't know what breaks when you move something. A dependency graph does. That's the difference."

*Screen: Drag it back. Warning disappears.*

---

### [1:25–1:45] FRESH GENERATION

*Screen: Navigate to /roadmap-builder. Select Finance → "Investment Banker". Major: Economics. Year: Sophomore. School: type "NYU" → select "New York University". Click "Build my roadmap". Loading spinner with progress labels: "Searching for real opportunities…" → "Cross-checking sources…" → "Sequencing dependencies…" → Dashboard appears with IB pipeline programs (Goldman Possibilities, SEO, Wall Street Scholars, etc.). Expand the chain on Wall Street Scholars to show: "Unlocks: Alumni referral network → Mock Superday eligibility → Sophomore internship interviews."*

> "From scratch. Sophomore, Econ, NYU, investment banking. Sylo builds the graph in ten seconds — Goldman Possibilities before SEO because its October deadline closes first and attending makes your SEO application stronger. Wall Street Scholars before Mock Superday because the simulator assumes you've done the technical workshops. The order isn't alphabetical. It's structural."

---

### [1:45–2:00] CLOSE

*Screen: Cut to a clean text card: "Degree planning models course prerequisites. Sylo models opportunity prerequisites." Sylo mark fades in.*

> "Degree planning already solved this for academics — it models course prerequisites as a graph and tells you what to take and when. Sylo applies the same structure to what comes after. Same student, same school, same data — pointed at career outcomes instead of graduation requirements."

> "The invisible advisor every well-connected student already has. Now available to everyone."

## PRODUCTION NOTES

1. **Use the Maya demo for the walkthrough** — she has a PURA Salary Award deadline on September 15 (verified from Georgia Tech's site), which triggers an amber urgency pill when you record in late August. Judges can click through and confirm the program is real.
2. **The dependency chain expansion is the new emotional peak.** The "three weeks away" moment is still powerful, but the *chain* — "Miss PURA and the next two steps become inaccessible" — is what makes a judge categorize this as an engine, not a directory. Pause the cursor on each row of the cascade panel for a full beat.
3. **The reorder warning is the proof moment.** This is where a technical judge leans forward. When the amber banner fires, let it sit on screen for 2 full seconds before dragging the step back. This single interaction proves the ordering is structural, not decorative.
4. **Expand at least two dependency chains** in the Maya walkthrough — one that shows "Requires: None" (Startup Lab) and one that shows "Requires: Completed Startup Lab" (Launch). The contrast makes the dependency logic undeniable.
5. **Use a fresh NYU generation for the "from scratch" beat** — and expand one dependency chain on the result to show it's not just pre-loaded data. The IB pipeline programs are all in the curated DB, so this will populate instantly.
6. **Say the words "dependency," "prerequisite," and "graph" out loud** in the voiceover. Judges categorize what they hear. If you only show it visually, they may still bucket it as "a nice list." The vocabulary forces correct categorization.
7. **The close text card changed.** "Degree planning models course prerequisites. Sylo models opportunity prerequisites." — stronger than the old version because it uses the same word (prerequisites) for both, making the parallel unmistakable.
8. **Record at 1920×1080**, browser zoomed to 115%. Light mode. No bookmarks bar.
9. **Slow mouse movements.** Let the cursor rest on each dependency chain row for half a beat before moving to the next.
10. **Record audio separately.** Clean room, close to mic, no background noise.
11. **Speed up loading spinners in post** — if anything takes >2 seconds, compress it to 1 second.
12. **No background music during voiceover.** Optional quiet music under the close only.
13. **Thumbnail:** Frame from the dashboard showing the expanded dependency chain with "Unlocks: Research Option credit → GRIP eligibility → Security clearance pipeline" visible.
14. **The first 8 seconds are everything.** The Reddit screenshot + "nobody told them it existed — or that it was a prerequisite for something else" plants the dependency framing from the very first line.
